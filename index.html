About the guide

Every week, thousands of Web3 conversations happen live on X Spaces. In each of them, a host decides who speaks, which questions are asked, and whether a misleading claim is challenged or left to spread.

This handbook exists because that role deserves real training. It takes a reader from “what is a Space?” to planning, opening, moderating and closing a professional Web3 Space, and shows how to handle the difficult moments, protect listeners from scams and misinformation, and turn each Space into lasting value.

It assumes no prior knowledge. Web3 terms are explained the first time they appear, and every important statement is labelled as a fact, best practice, recommendation or opinion, so readers always know what kind of claim they are reading. Platform details were checked against X’s official Help Center in October 2026.

What you will learn

Twelve areas of skill, from the first decision about a Space to growing a reputation as a host.

Planning
Define an objective, audience, key message and format before inviting anyone.
Preparation
Brief speakers in writing, build an agenda and prepare your technology.
Questioning
Ask questions that produce specific, useful answers, including difficult ones.
Moderation
Balance speaking time, interrupt professionally and keep the conversation moving.
Etiquette
Clear standards for hosts, co-hosts, speakers, listeners and project teams.
Audience management
Collect questions, bring listeners on stage and manage quiet or crowded rooms.
Web3 safety
Recognise phishing, impersonation, fake giveaways and misleading financial claims.
Difficult situations
Respond calmly to arguments, trolls, harassment and technical failures.
Promotion
Announce, schedule and remind without overhyping.
Post-Space follow-up
Close well, thank speakers and turn one Space into a week of content.
Performance measurement
Measure reach, depth, value and community, not listener count alone.
Professional growth
Build credibility, relationships and a hosting portfolio over time.

Key frameworks

Short, memorable structures you can carry into any Space.

The 5P Framework The backbone of every Space

  1. PurposeWhy this Space exists and what should change
  2. PeopleThe audience, and the speakers who serve it
  3. PreparationBriefing, questions, agenda, tech checks, promotion
  4. PerformanceOpening, moderation and closing
  5. Post-SpaceFollow-up, content and evaluation

The 3S Framework Good moderation, live

  1. StructureA clear plan, agenda and rules everyone understands
  2. SteeringLive decisions about speakers, follow-ups and direction
  3. SafetyProtecting people and truth from abuse, scams and misinformation

The guide also teaches the ACT technique for interrupting professionally (Acknowledge, Connect, Transfer), the Correction Ladder for misinformation (Clarify, Contextualise, Correct), the CALM response for difficult moments, and the Question Ladder for building depth.
